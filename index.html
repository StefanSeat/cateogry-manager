Know the company

Eurospin is an Italian discount supermarket chain built on a short assortment and a strong private label. Check the latest figures on the official site before the interview.

1993

Founded in Italy (headquartered in San Martino Buon Albergo, Verona) by a group of retail families.

1,300+

Stores, mostly in Italy, plus Slovenia, Croatia and Malta. One of the largest discounters in Italy.

~80%

Rough share of private label in the assortment. Own brands are the core of the model, not an add-on.

"La spesa intelligente"

The slogan: smart shopping. Quality at a low price, with Italian origin as a big part of the story.

How the model makes money

  • Limited assortment (a few thousand SKUs vs. 15,000+ in a classic supermarket)
  • High volume per SKU, so strong buying power per item
  • Private label with direct producer relationships
  • Lean stores: simple layout, shelf-ready packaging, low operating cost
  • Weekly non-food and promo flyer to drive traffic

Competitors to mention

  • Lidl: the main discount rival in every Eurospin market
  • Aldi (Hofer in Slovenia): strong in Slovenia
  • MD Discount, Penny, Todis: in Italy
  • Konzum, Spar, Tommy, Mercator: classic supermarkets in Croatia and Slovenia

Private label brands

Eurospin runs many own brands across categories (fresh, dairy, bakery, drinks, household, personal care, pet). Walk a store before the interview and note 5 to 10 of them, with prices.

Do this before the interview: visit one Eurospin and one Lidl. Compare 10 basket items (milk, pasta, olive oil, coffee, eggs, chicken, yogurt, detergent, beer, chocolate) on price, pack size, shelf presentation and availability. Bring those notes.

The role

At a discounter the category manager is mostly a buyer and a P&L owner. Fewer SKUs means every listing decision matters more.

Assortment

Decide what is on the shelf. Add, delist, replace. Keep the range short and clear: usually one or two options per need.

Suppliers and negotiation

Tenders for private label, annual price talks, cost increases, payment terms, logistics conditions, quality specs.

Pricing

Hold price leadership versus Lidl and the market on key value items. Protect margin on the rest.

Promotions

Plan the flyer, special buys and seasonal items (Christmas, Easter, summer, back to school).

Private label development

Brief new products, test them, benchmark against the brand leader, follow quality controls.

Performance

Weekly sales, margin, stock, waste. Act fast on what is not working.

Category management process

The classic 8 steps. Good structure for any "how would you manage category X" question.

  1. Define the category. What need does it cover, from the shopper's point of view? (Example: "breakfast" vs. just "cereals")
  2. Give it a role. Destination, routine, seasonal or convenience. In a discounter most core food is "routine", and price-sensitive items are traffic builders.
  3. Assess it. Sales, margin, market share, trends, competitor ranges and prices, shopper data.
  4. Set a scorecard. Concrete targets: sales growth, gross margin %, availability, waste %, stock days.
  5. Pick a strategy. Traffic, transactions (basket size), profit, cash, image, excitement.
  6. Choose tactics. Assortment, pricing, promotion, shelf layout, supply chain.
  7. Implement. Listings, supplier agreements, planograms, store communication.
  8. Review. Measure against the scorecard, adjust, repeat.

KPIs and formulas

Expect at least one numbers question. Know these cold.

Gross margin %(Sales − COGS) / SalesMain profitability measure of a category
Markup %(Price − Cost) / CostNot the same as margin. 25% markup = 20% margin
Sales per SKUCategory sales / number of SKUsShows whether the range is too wide
Stock turnoverCOGS / average stockDiscounters need high rotation
Days of stockAverage stock / daily COGSCash tied up and freshness risk
GMROIGross margin / average stock at costProfit per unit of money invested in stock
Waste / shrinkage %Losses / salesCritical in fresh (fruit and veg, bakery, meat)
Availability (OSA)% of time the product is on the shelfEmpty shelf = lost sale, and a lost customer at a discounter
Promo upliftPromo sales − baseline salesWas the promo worth the margin you gave away?
Price indexOur price / competitor price × 100Below 100 on key value items versus Lidl
Quick practice: a product costs 1.60 and sells for 2.00 net of VAT. Margin = 0.40 / 2.00 = 20%. Markup = 0.40 / 1.60 = 25%. Supplier asks for +8% on cost: new cost 1.728. To keep 20% margin the price goes to 2.16. If you hold 2.00 the margin drops to 13.6%.

Likely questions

Tap a question for an answer outline. Use STAR for experience questions: Situation, Task, Action, Result (with a number).

Why Eurospin?Motivation

Combine three things: the discount model (efficiency, every SKU counts, you see your impact fast), private label (you shape the product, not just buy it), and growth (the chain keeps opening stores outside Italy).

Add something personal: you shop there, you noticed a specific product or price you liked.

What does a category manager do, in your words?Basics

Runs a category like a small business: decides the range, buys it at the best conditions, sets the price and promo plan, and is responsible for sales, margin and stock. At a discounter, with a short range and a lot of private label, it is closer to "product owner and buyer" than at a classic retailer.

A supplier wants a 10% price increase. What do you do?Negotiation

1) Ask for the cost breakdown and check it against raw material indexes (for example milk, wheat, energy). 2) Know your alternatives: other producers, tender, different spec or pack size. 3) Separate the justified part from the rest. 4) Trade, don't give: if you accept part of it, get something back (longer validity, promo support, payment terms, volume commitment). 5) Calculate the impact on retail price and on the price index versus Lidl before you agree.

Sales in a category fell 12%. How do you analyse it?Analysis

Split the drop: is it the market, or us? Then volume vs. price, then which SKUs and which stores. Check availability (out of stocks), competitor promos and price changes, delistings, seasonality and weather, shelf space changes. Then act on the biggest driver first and set a date to measure again.

How would you decide which SKU to delist?Assortment

Rank by sales, margin contribution and rotation. Then check whether it is unique: if customers would switch to another item in the range (high transferability), delist it. If it is the only answer to a need, or brings people into the store, keep it even with low numbers. Watch out for items that support a basket (for example a specific spice for a popular dish).

Private label vs. brands: how do you balance them?Strategy

At Eurospin private label leads. Brands earn a place when they are a must-have the shopper would walk out for (a few iconic items), or to show price credibility next to the private label. The private label should match the brand leader on quality in blind tests and beat it clearly on price.

How do you develop a new private label product?Private label

Gap in the range or a growing trend, then benchmark the brand leader (spec, price, pack). Write the spec, run a tender with 3 to 5 producers, test samples (blind tasting, lab), agree price and terms, design the pack, launch, then track sales, repeat purchase and complaints for the first 12 weeks.

How do you know if a promotion worked?Promo

Compare to the baseline: incremental units, incremental margin (not just sales), effect on the rest of the category (did it just steal from other SKUs?), sales after the promo (dip from stockpiling), and traffic. A promo that adds sales but loses margin and steals from your own range is not a win.

Tell me about a difficult negotiation.STAR

Prepare one real story with a number in the result. Show that you prepared (data, alternatives), stayed calm, and found a deal where both sides got something. End with what you learned.

Tell me about a decision you made with data.STAR

Pick a case where the data pointed against the first opinion. Say which data, what you concluded, what you did, and the measurable result.

What would you change in a Eurospin store you visited?Store visit

Use your store visit notes. Be constructive: one thing they do well, one or two concrete ideas (for example a gap in the range, a price that is above Lidl, a shelf that was empty). Show you think like the customer and like the P&L owner at the same time.

Where do you see yourself in 3 to 5 years?Motivation

Owning a bigger or more strategic category (for example fresh), with proven results in margin and private label growth, and possibly leading a small team. Keep it tied to Eurospin.

30 / 60 / 90 day plan

If they ask "how would you start?", answer with this.

First 30 days: learn

  • Learn the systems, reports and the calendar (promo, negotiations)
  • Meet every key supplier in the category
  • Visit stores and warehouses, talk to store managers
  • Map the category: SKUs, sales, margins, price index

Days 31 to 60: diagnose

  • Find the weak SKUs and the gaps in the range
  • Check prices versus Lidl and the market
  • Review supplier terms and upcoming cost increases
  • Agree targets with the manager

Days 61 to 90: act

  • First delistings and new listings
  • One private label improvement or new product brief
  • First negotiations with clear goals
  • Promo plan for the next season

Questions to ask them

Pick 3 or 4. They show you think about the business, not just the job.

  • Which categories would I manage, and what is their role in the strategy (traffic, margin, growth)?
  • How are buying decisions split between the local team and headquarters in Italy?
  • How much of the range in this market is sourced locally versus from the central Italian range?
  • What KPIs will I be measured on in the first year?
  • What is the biggest challenge in this category right now?
  • How do you work with private label producers on quality control?
  • What does the team look like, and who would I work with most?
  • What does success look like in this role after 12 months?
