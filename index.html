    Account Development Playbook

    One shared set of rules, so every lead is handled the same way, reports stay comparable, and new hires get productive fast. This is the organisation side of the role: tool training, onboarding and reporting protocols.

    Lead stages and exit criteria

    1

    New

    Lead created from marketing or research. Scored automatically.

    Exit: first touch logged.

    2

    Working

    Analyst researching and making contact.

    Exit: fit confirmed and a reply or meeting interest.

    3

    MQL

    Marketing qualified: right profile, real interest.

    Exit: need, timing and service line identified.

    4

    SQL

    AD accepts the hand-off and owns the relationship.

    Exit: RFP or RFI received.

    5

    RFP

    Proposal in progress with Business Development.

    Exit: awarded, lost or withdrawn.

    Response SLAs
    • RFI form or referral: first response within 4 business hours
    • Grade A lead: to an AD within 24 hours
    • Grade B lead: first nurture touch within 3 business days
    • Grade C lead: marketing nurture, re-score monthly
    CRM hygiene rules
    • Every touch logged the same day (call, email, meeting)
    • Required fields before hand-off: service line, phase, area, region, next step, next step date
    • One account, one owner. Search before creating to avoid duplicates
    • Disqualify with a reason code (no budget, in-house, competitor locked, wrong fit)
    • Friday: 15-minute data check before the weekly report
    Hand-off standard
    • Who: contact, role, others engaged
    • What: trial phase, area, service interest
    • Why now: funding, new trial, RFI, event
    • Next step agreed with the prospect
    • Warm intro by email, AD in copy

    Prospect research checklist

    1. Pipeline: company website pipeline page and ClinicalTrials.gov (registered trials, phase, start dates, countries).
    2. Funding: press releases and financing news. A new round often means a new trial within 6 to 18 months.
    3. People: LinkedIn for the decision makers (VP Clinical Operations, Head of Outsourcing, CMO) and recent hires in clinical roles.
    4. Signals: conference posters, regulatory designations (orphan, fast track), partnership announcements.
    5. History: CRM check for past contact, earlier proposals or existing work with any Thermo Fisher business.

    New hire onboarding plan (AD team)

    Week 1: tools
    • CRM and sales automation set-up, views and dashboards
    • Lead stages, SLAs and this playbook
    • Shadow lead triage for two mornings
    Week 2: offer
    • CRO services overview: clinical, regulatory, preclinical, data, labs
    • Therapeutic area one-pagers
    • Listen to 3 discovery calls
    Week 3: practice
    • Research 10 accounts with the checklist
    • Write 5 hand-off notes, reviewed by a peer
    • First outreach sequence live
    Week 4: own it
    • Own a small territory or lead queue
    • First weekly report
    • 30-day check-in: what is unclear, what to fix in the playbook

    Know the company

    Thermo Fisher Scientific

    World leader in serving science, with 100,000+ colleagues. Mission: enable customers to make the world healthier, cleaner and safer. Values: Integrity, Intensity, Innovation, Involvement (the 4i).

    PPD

    Global CRO founded in 1985, acquired by Thermo Fisher in December 2021. Now the clinical research business of Thermo Fisher, part of the Clinical Research Group (CRG).

    CRG services
    • Clinical development, Phase I to IV
    • Regulatory affairs
    • Preclinical studies
    • Data management and biostatistics
    • Laboratory services (central, bioanalytical, vaccine)
    • Functional service provision (FSP)
    Competitors
    • Full-service CROs: IQVIA, ICON, Parexel, Syneos Health, Fortrea
    • Biotech focused: Medpace
    • Preclinical: Charles River

    Edge to mention: one partner from molecule to market, including Thermo Fisher's labs, manufacturing and clinical supply.

    CRO vocabulary

    Sponsor: the pharma or biotech company paying for the trial.

    RFI / RFP: request for information / proposal. The RFP is the start of a bid.

    Bid defense: the meeting where the CRO presents its proposal to the sponsor.

    CDA / MSA: confidentiality agreement / master services agreement.

    FSP vs full service: CRO provides staff for specific functions, or runs the whole trial.

    IND: the filing needed to start human trials in the US.

    MQL / SQL: marketing / sales qualified lead.

    Emerging biopharma: small biotechs with no big internal teams, who outsource most development. A key target group.

    Phases: I safety, II efficacy and dose, III large confirmation, IV after approval.

    Likely questions

    Why this role, and why a CRO?Motivation

    I like the commercial side when it is backed by data: finding the right customers, measuring what works, making the team more effective. At a CRO that work helps get new medicines to patients faster, which makes the numbers matter more. Thermo Fisher's mission (healthier, cleaner, safer) is a clear reason to show up.

    You have 50 new leads on Monday. How do you prioritise?Lead mgmt

    Score them on fit, pipeline, intent and engagement (exactly what the Lead Desk does). RFIs and referrals first, within hours. Grade A to the ADs the same day with a hand-off note. Grade B into targeted nurture. Grade C stays with Marketing. Then I check the SLA list at the end of the day.

    How would you research a prospective client?Demand gen

    Pipeline page and ClinicalTrials.gov for phase and timing, funding news, LinkedIn for decision makers, CRM history. I look for a "why now": a new round, a trial moving to the next phase, a regulatory designation.

    What makes a good hand-off to an AD?Collaboration

    The AD should not need to redo my research. Who, what, why now, what was agreed with the prospect, and a warm intro. The Lead Desk generates this note automatically.

    How would you train the team on a new tool?Training

    Short sessions built on real daily tasks, a one-page cheat sheet, a shared playbook, and office hours in the first weeks. Then I measure adoption (fields filled, reports used) and fix what people struggle with.

    Which metrics would you report weekly?Reporting

    Lead volume by source, conversion at each stage (lead to MQL, MQL to SQL, SQL to RFP), response time against SLA, RFP count, and stalled leads. Always the same format, so trends are visible.

    Excel or CRM skills?Tools

    Pivot tables, XLOOKUP / VLOOKUP, conditional formatting, data cleaning, charts for reports. In CRM: views, dashboards, data hygiene. This site is the proof that I can turn messy data into a clear tool.
